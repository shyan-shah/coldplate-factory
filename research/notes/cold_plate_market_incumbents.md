# Direct-to-Chip Liquid Cooling and Cold Plates for AI Data Centres: Market, Buyers, Prices, Incumbents (research as of 2026-11-19)

Provenance convention: "(fetched)" means the page was retrieved and read. "(search summary, page not fetched)" means the claim comes from a search-engine summary of the page and was not verified against the full text. Treat the second kind as lower confidence.

## 1. Market size and growth (liquid cooling overall, DLC, cold plates), 2024-2030

### Takeaway
Analyst scopes differ so much that the figures cannot be blended. The most conservative credible anchor for all data-centre liquid cooling is Dell'Oro: about $3B of manufacturer revenue in 2025, rising to about $7B by 2029. The only bank-sourced estimate for cold plates alone is Bernstein (July 2026, via a secondary write-up): $2-3B today and $6-7B by 2030. That puts cold plates alone at roughly the size of Dell'Oro's whole liquid-cooling market, a sign that the two use different scopes (see Inferences). Vendor-report cold plate figures range from $252M to $4.1B for 2025.

### Inferences
- (Inference) Dell'Oro's ~$3B for all liquid cooling in 2025 is almost certainly narrower than the actual spend on server-level cold plates. AVC alone had about $4.5B of 2025 revenue, driven "largely by liquid cooling modules". Delta's liquid cooling was about NT$48bn (~$1.5B) in 2025. Boyd forecasts $1.5B of liquid-cooling sales for 2026, and CoolIT about $550M over the next 12 months. Dell'Oro appears to capture mainly DCPI-channel vendors (CDUs, RDHx, facility-side) rather than ODM-channel cold plates and manifolds bought by Taiwanese ODMs. This mixes years and includes CDUs and manifolds, so it is directional only.
- (Inference) A conservative planning figure for the cold plate component alone is about $2-3B in 2025-26, growing to $6-7B by 2030 (Bernstein). Mordor's $4.1B is the upper bound. Sub-$700M figures probably exclude AI-server ODM-channel plates or use outdated bases.
- (Inference) Bernstein's commoditisation warning and the "design-driven, manufacturing outsourced" framing bear directly on the thesis. Design is where value sits early in a generation, and pricing compresses once a design matures.

## 2. Who buys cold plates, how, and who controls the spec

### Takeaway
For NVIDIA rack-scale platforms, cold plates are bought as part of the server or rack, through ODMs (Foxconn, Quanta, Wiwynn) and OEMs (Supermicro, Dell), from a short NVIDIA- or CSP-approved list dominated by Taiwanese thermal firms. Control is tightening. For Vera Rubin (2H26), NVIDIA reportedly standardised the liquid cooling, named four cold plate suppliers (AVC, Cooler Master, Jentech, Delta) and centralised cold plate procurement. It is also reported to be moving toward shipping fully assembled L10 compute trays. Hyperscaler custom ASICs (TPU, Trainium, MTIA) are the main area where cold plate specs are set outside NVIDIA, and they still flow to the same Taiwanese incumbents.

### Inferences
- (Inference) On NVIDIA flagship racks, an independent startup is unlikely to win cold plate business without being on NVIDIA's or a CSP's qualified list, and the Vera Rubin list is reportedly closed at four. Any NVIDIA flagship-rack opportunity would be second-sourcing or follow-on generations, not open per-unit sales.
- (Inference) The more open segments are custom ASIC racks (Broadcom- and Marvell-designed XPUs, Trainium, MTIA, TPU), AMD Helios and MI-series OEM boards, enterprise and HGX/MGX OEM servers (Dell, Supermicro, HPE, Lenovo) and non-GPU components (DIMMs, NICs, optics, power boards). But hyperscalers buy these through the same incumbent Taiwanese suppliers, so incumbency is strong there too.
- (Inference) Colocation operators and cooling integrators mostly buy facility-side gear (CDUs, secondary fluid networks), not server cold plates. Cold plates arrive inside the server.

## 3. Volumes and prices (per cold plate, per rack)

### Takeaway
A leaked Morgan Stanley note puts GB300 NVL72 liquid-cooling content at about $49,860 per rack and Vera Rubin at about $55,710. It prices GPU/CPU cold plates at about $300 each and NVSwitch plates at about $200. KGI flags GPU and CPU cold plate pricing pressure for Vera Rubin in 2H26 even as content per rack rises. Unit capacities are large: Auras alone is expanding to 300k cold plates per month.

### Inferences
- (Inference, illustrative only) At about $300 per plate and roughly 100 plates per rack, GPU and CPU cold plates are about $30k per GB300 rack. That is most of the ~$50k liquid-cooling content; the remainder is QDs, manifolds and switch-tray plates. Plate-count definitions differ (KGI "modules" versus Bernstein "one per chip"), so this is a rough decomposition.
- (Inference) Flagship-platform cold plates are a roughly $200-400 volume part sold to a few buyers under pricing pressure. A per-unit custom model would need either a premium for performance or speed, or higher-mix, lower-volume segments where incumbents' scale matters less.
- (Inference) Capacity benchmark: Auras' stage-1 300k per month is about 3.6M plates a year at one supplier. Corintis' stated 1M-per-year target would be well below a single incumbent's run-rate.

## 4. Incumbents: large players and startups (capacity, expansion, revenue, lead times)

### Takeaway
The market is consolidating around very large, well-capitalised players. Taiwanese thermal firms (AVC, Auras, Cooler Master, Delta) dominate NVIDIA cold plate volume. Western industrials are buying in at high multiples: Eaton bought Boyd Thermal for $9.5B, Ecolab bought CoolIT for $4.75B, and Schneider bought Motivair for about $850M for 75%, with roughly $1B total. Two-phase and microfluidic startups have raised $65-100M rounds.

### Cited Findings
**AVC (Asia Vital Components)**
- 2025 revenue was about US$4.5B, "nearly double" the prior year and "driven largely by liquid cooling modules". It holds an estimated 40-50% of the NVIDIA GB200/GB300 cold plate market. It is expanding cold plate, manifold and chassis capacity in Vietnam, with "a US footprint under evaluation", and holds more than 1,700 thermal patents. (fetched, Apr 2026) — [t.media/TVBS](https://t.media/3183921)

(search summary / fetched respectively) — [TrendForce news via search](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2025/03/10/news-nvidia-to-unveil-gb300-at-gtc-with-shipment-reportedly-to-begin-in-may-driving-cooling-demands/); [Taiwan News, Sep 2025](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/news/6209780)

## 5. Evidence on whether custom cold plate design speed is a pain point

### Takeaway
I found no buyer-side statement (OEM, ODM, hyperscaler or colo) that cold plate design speed is a bottleneck. The documented constraints are: quick-disconnect shortages (2024), CDU lead times, factory yield and capacity ramps (for example Auras in Thailand), supplier-list access, and pricing pressure. There is evidence of growing customisation demand: hyperscaler ASICs, chip-specific microchannel and hotspot designs, liquid cooling spreading to more board components, and NVIDIA's MCL/MCCP work. But that demand is being met by incumbents and well-funded specialists.

### Cited Findings
- Supply constraints: Supermicro's ~$800M revenue delay from a QD shortage (Aug 2024; QD prices rose from $40 to $60). (search summary) — [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/shortage-of-quick-couplings-for-liquid-cooling-costs-supermicro-800-million-in-delayed-revenue). Auras' 4Q25 inventory impairment and scrap loss at its Thailand factory, and 2026 growth "backed by improving efficiency and yield rate at the Thailand factory". (fetched) — [KGI Auras](https://www.kgi.com.hk/en/-/media/files/kgishk/research-reports/tw-reports/auras-technology_3324-tt_20260330e.pdf)
- Customisation demand: liquid cooling is spreading on Vera Rubin to CX9 NICs, busbars and power boards, and optical transceivers. (fetched) — [TrendForce, Aug 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260817-13183.html). GB, VR, CPU, storage and networking racks all feature cold plates, ASIC liquid-cooling demand is growing, and HGX DIMM liquid cooling adds to demand. (fetched) — [KGI Auras](https://www.kgi.com.hk/en/-/media/files/kgishk/research-reports/tw-reports/auras-technology_3324-tt_20260330e.pdf)
- New ASIC designs: Trainium 2.5 and 3 move from air to liquid. (fetched) — [KGI, Oct 2025](https://www.kgi.com.hk/en/-/media/files/kgishk/research-reports/tw-reports/thermal-sector_27102025.pdf). Broadcom XPU cooling with JetCool; Bernie Malouin: "Cooling has become a primary design constraint for next-generation AI silicon". (search summary) — [StorageNewsletter](https://www.storagenewsletter.com/2026/03/17/nvidia-gtc-2026-jetcool-collaborates-with-broadcom-to-deliver-innovative-liquid-cooling-for-next-generation-ai-xpus/)
- In Sep 2025, Rubin qualification was weighing cold plate versus microchannel lid "in the coming 1-2 months". This shows platform-level design decisions happen on short, NVIDIA-driven timelines. (search summary of a conference transcript, page not fetched) — [Reportify transcript](https://reportify.cn/transcripts/1167446421666926592)
- Chip-specific design rationale (vendor voice, not buyer voice): Corintis' CEO says "every chip is unique" and "Cooling today is not adapted to the chip". - One analyst note says hyperscaler buying decisions are gated less by lab performance than by who can source, build and ship complete systems reliably across regions. (search summary of Sacra; low reliability) — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/a7742ed5-6789-40cf-9d3c-1c407eae5ea1/)

### Inferences
- (Inference) The evidence supports "customisation demand is rising", from more chip types, more cooled components and higher TDPs. It does not support "design speed is the buyer's binding constraint". Buyers' stated pain is supply security, capacity, yield, leak reliability (QDs) and qualification.
- (Inference) Where design does matter (NVIDIA MCL/MCCP, Google TPU, Trainium), it is done in close co-design with chip owners and existing qualified suppliers, often before tape-out. That favours embedded engineering relationships over a per-unit catalogue model.

### Gaps
- No buyer interviews, surveys or procurement quotes on design-cycle time. This needs primary research, such as interviews with ODM thermal leads or hyperscaler hardware teams.
- No sourced durations for cold plate qualification at NVIDIA or CSPs.

## 6. Corintis (Lausanne): what it does, funding, customers, design-automation and manufacturing approach

### Takeaway
Corintis is the closest existing analogue to the "AI-designed, custom cold plate factory" idea. It is an EPFL spin-off (founded 2022) that combines generative design software (Glacierware) with its own copper microfluidic cold plate manufacturing and a silicon test-chip platform (Therminator). It has raised about $58M (Series A of $24M in Sep 2025 and Series A1 of $25M in Dec 2025, led by Applied Digital). Its publicly validated reference customer is Microsoft. It sells drop-in replacement cold plates today and targets in-chip and co-packaged cooling later.

### Cited Findings
- Sep 25, 2025: $24M Series A led by BlueYard, with Founderful, Acequia Capital, Celsius Industries and XTX Ventures. Total funding to that point was $33.4M. Lip-Bu Tan (Intel CEO) joined the board and invested. Geoff Lyon (CoolIT founder and former CEO) joined the board. (fetched) — [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2025/09/25/corintis-lands-24m-and-microsoft-endorsement-for-chip-cooling-tech/)
- Product and platform: microfluidic cooling sold as a drop-in replacement for existing liquid cooling or as "co-packaged cooling". Glacierware "automates cooling system design". It has a copper microfluidic manufacturing facility, and Therminator emulates next-generation chips on silicon test chips. (fetched) — [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2025/09/25/corintis-lands-24m-and-microsoft-endorsement-for-chip-cooling-tech/)
- Scale: more than 10,000 cooling systems manufactured, with a target of more than 1 million microfluidic cold plates a year "by 2026". Cumulative revenue is "eight-digit" (company claim) and expected to grow more than 10x with early deployments. Customers include "several major American tech companies", with deployments on leading-edge AI chips. Headcount was 55, planned to exceed 70 by end-2025. It has US offices and a Munich engineering office. (fetched) — [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2025/09/25/corintis-lands-24m-and-microsoft-endorsement-for-chip-cooling-tech/)
- CEO to Reuters: "Right now we are able to produce around 100,000 cold plates per year. Next year we are ramping up to around 1 million cold plates per year." (search summary, page not fetched) — [The Star/Reuters](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/09/25/chip-cooling-startup-corintis-raises-24-million-adds-intel-ceo-lip-bu-tan-to-board)
- Dec 15, 2025: $25M Series A1 led by Applied Digital (a data-centre operator), bringing total funding to $58M. It describes "a generative design and manufacturing platform" that tailors cooling architectures to individual chips and claims up to 3x lower chip temperatures than conventional liquid cooling. Multiple unnamed global technology customers have signed since the Series A, and a US office is planned in Washington. (fetched) — [GGBA](https://ggba.swiss/en/corintis-secures-additional-usd-25-million-to-scale-advanced-chip-cooling-technology/). Microsoft's Husam Alissa cited the thermal margin for overclocking and 3-D chip architectures. (fetched) — [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2025/09/25/corintis-lands-24m-and-microsoft-endorsement-for-chip-cooling-tech/); [Data Center Frontier](https://www.datacenterfrontier.com/cooling/article/55326929/microsofts-in-chip-microfluidics-technology-resets-the-limits-of-ai-cooling)

### Inferences
- (Inference) Corintis already occupies the "AI-designed, chip-specific cold plate plus in-house manufacturing" position. It has about $58M, Microsoft validation, a data-centre-operator strategic investor (Applied Digital) and board members from Intel and CoolIT. A new entrant would need a clear differentiator, such as robotic or lights-out manufacturing cost, segment focus (ASIC, enterprise, edge, non-GPU components), or speed.
- (Inference) Corintis' even-stated 1M-per-year ambition is small next to a single Taiwanese incumbent (Auras at about 3.6M per year in stage 1). The volume NVIDIA flagship market is not where a custom-design player wins on scale.
