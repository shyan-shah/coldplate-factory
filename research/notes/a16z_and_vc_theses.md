Labelling: "[fetched]" means the primary or near-primary page was fetched and read. "[secondary, not fetched]" means the claim comes only from search-result summaries or aggregators and should be verified before external use.

**Summaries of the relevant AD ideas.** All come from [a16z.news Part 2, 10 Dec 2025](https://www.a16z.news/p/big-ideas-2026-part-2) [fetched].
- **#15 Ulevitch, "Building the AI-native industrial base".** The next industrial base will be AI-native and software-first (paraphrase), starting with simulation, automated design and AI-driven operations. Named opportunity areas are advanced energy, robotics-heavy manufacturing, next-generation mining, and enzymatic precursor chemicals. He also describes autonomous sensors and drones monitoring ports, rail, power lines, pipelines and datacenters. Quotes: "AI can design cleaner reactors, optimize extraction, engineer better enzymes, and coordinate fleets of autonomous machines"; "They are not modernizing the past. They are building what comes next."; "The real world needs new software."
- **#16 Price-Wright, "The renaissance of the American factory".** Energy, mining, construction and manufacturing companies should adopt a factory mindset (paraphrase). That means modular deployment of AI and autonomy alongside skilled workers, designing for scale and repeatability from day zero (paraphrase), and faster permitting and design cycles. She says: "we'll soon be mass-producing nuclear reactors, building housing that meets our nation's demand", and datacenters are also named as mass-produced. Further quotes: "To quote Elon Musk, 'the factory is the product.'"; "Deploy autonomy to speed up tasks that are difficult or dangerous for humans to manage."
- **#17 Elmgren, physical observability.** Over a billion networked cameras and sensors in US cities make real-time understanding of cities, grids and infrastructure "both urgent and possible". The winners will build "privacy-preserving, interoperable, AI-native systems".
- **#18 McEntush, "The electro-industrial stack".** The stack is materials, batteries, power electronics, motors and software behind EVs, drones, datacenters and modern manufacturing. He warns that US refining and chip-fabrication capacity is eroding. Quotes: "Software ate the world. Now it will move it."; "The nations that master the electro-industrial stack will define the future of industrial and military technologies."
- **#19 Hsu, "Autonomous labs".** Multimodal models plus robotic manipulation will "close the loop on scientific discovery" through "lights-out labs". Teams will combine AI, robotics, the sciences and manufacturing.
- **#20 Bitsky, "Data crusade in our critical industries".** In 2026 the AI constraint shifts from compute to process data. "Each truck roll, meter read, maintenance job, production run, assembly, and test fire is fodder for model training." He expects startups to supply collection, annotation, consent, sensors and RL-environment tooling, and eventually their own intelligent machines.

### Inferences
- (Inference) The cold-plate autonomous-factory concept maps onto #15 (robotics-heavy, AI-designed manufacturing), #16 (factory as product, designed for scale from day zero), #18 (the datacenter is part of the electro-industrial stack) and #20 (production-run and test data as a moat). The pitch can quote these ideas directly.
- (Inference) No Big Ideas 2026 item mentions liquid cooling, thermal management or cold plates. The datacenter references are about mass-producing datacenters, monitoring them, or treating them as part of the electro-industrial stack. They are not about components.
- (Inference) a16z's December cadence makes a 2027 edition likely around 9-11 Dec 2026, roughly three weeks after this note. Re-check before finalising any report.

## Q2. a16z American Dynamism theses on manufacturing, robotics, defence, energy and compute buildout

### Takeaway
a16z's 2026 output moved decisively from prototypes to production. Four pieces carry the message:
- The AD50 2026 (29 Oct 2026) is themed "Production is Deterrence".
- "The Case for the American Manufacturing Asset Class" (2 Oct 2026) calls Tier 2/3 suppliers and machine shops "grossly underpriced".
- Keil's "Superhuman Hardware Design Is The Easy Part" (20 Oct 2026) argues that design for manufacturing (DFM), scaling and process data, not design, are the bottlenecks.
- The $1.1B Machine Age Fund (28 Aug 2026) targets the physical layer of AI, including "cooling".

### Cited Findings
**AD50 2026: "Production is Deterrence" (29 Oct 2026, a16z American Dynamism team)** [fetched] — [a16z AD50 2026](https://a16z.com/american-dynamism-50-2026/)
- The list is organised in three pillars: Inputs (11), Factories (16) and Systems (23).
- Quotes: "There is no scaled defense industrial base without a strong industrial base."; "Strength is measured by the ability to keep producing under pressure."; "Founders must design for mass production from day one, weighing cost and manufacturability alongside performance."
- Stated drivers are Russia's war in Ukraine, US munitions stockpile gaps tied to the conflict with Iran, and China's rare-earth export restrictions.
- The list covers companies "from within the a16z portfolio and beyond" — [a16z on X](https://x.com/a16z/status/2115807122365845729). The page itself does not mark which entries are a16z portfolio and gives no selection methodology. Its disclaimer says "Any investments or portfolio companies mentioned... are not representative" — [a16z AD50 2026](https://a16z.com/american-dynamism-50-2026/) [fetched, second pass]
- Dealroom reads the list as a shift from funding novel prototypes to manufacturing at scale. It also cautions that the list is an investor-curated ecosystem list and not proof that the bottlenecks are solved — [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/note/a16z-s-american-dynamism-50-reframes-defense-around-factories-not-prototypes) [secondary, not fetched]
- The full company list is given under Q3.

**"The Case for the American Manufacturing Asset Class" (Connor Love and Collen Larson, 2 Oct 2026)** [fetched] — [a16z](https://a16z.com/the-case-for-the-american-manufacturing-asset-class/)
- Thesis: defence demand is shifting from prototypes to production, and the industrial base under defence startups is underfunded. Quote: "Without it, America ends up with prototypes instead of an overwhelming force capable of deterrence."
- Quotes on the opportunity: "Silicon Valley has largely overlooked that base"; "a generational opportunity for American capital to build enduring manufacturing companies"; "America cannot compete on labor cost, nor should we try."
- Targets: Tier 2/3 suppliers and component makers, the layer "technology hasn't touched", machine shops, process specialists, firms that co-engineer with systems companies, and multi-program suppliers that avoid sole-source dependence.
- Valuation claim: "Tier 2 and 3 suppliers... sit grossly underpriced"; "the long tail has barely been touched".
- Capital intensity: "Manufacturing is capital-in, capability-out."; "There is no 'silver bullet' software-style zero-marginal-cost escape hatch." Software helps only when it addresses what physically limits the line.
- Financing: venture takes early technical risk, then growth equity, PE and credit fund expansion. "expensive venture equity becomes the wrong tool for expansion." The essay says the Pentagon should act as anchor customer.
- Examples:
  - Hadrian: its Opus platform turns design files into manufacturing steps, with "spaceflight-grade parts 10x faster", "$1.37 billion in equity" and a "$360 million revolving credit facility".
  - Amca: its RAPID platform ships "more than 50,000 components each month" and cut development-to-production time by 67%.
  - Anduril, Castelion and Nominal are also cited.

**"Superhuman Hardware Design Is The Easy Part" (Christian Keil, AD partner, 20 Oct 2026)** [fetched] — [a16z](https://a16z.com/superhuman-hardware-design-is-the-easy-part/)
- "Superhuman hardware engineers are coming soon." Frontier models already generate CAD, design PCBs and source parts.
- DFM "requires significantly more interaction with the real world". The digital-physical interface is lossy (paraphrase).
- On simulation: "better simulation tools would allow hardware engineers to iterate at software speed"; "Atoms are likely to stay in the iteration loop forever." Physical builds become "confirmatory, not experimental".
- On scale: "It is a fundamentally different task to build 1 of something and to build 1,000,000 of something." The needs are process-variability control, upfront financing, workforce and vendor capacity.
- Opportunities: demand will "shift left" to raw materials and component suppliers as "more companies bring more of their bill of materials in house". Process-knowledge data is scarce and valuable.
- Named companies:
  - Simulation: Navier, Inviscid, Lanyon.
  - Manufacturing and supply: Senra, Podium Automation, Hadrian, Cofactr, Diode, Mariana Minerals.
  - Test and data: Sift, Instrumental, Revel.

**The Machine Age Fund ($1.1B; Ben Horowitz, Martin Casado, Raghu Raghuram, David Ulevitch, David George; 28 Aug 2026)** [fetched] — [a16z](https://a16z.com/the-machine-age-fund/)
- Aim: "open the throttle and accelerate the physical buildout of AI". "Machine intelligence is going vertical."
- Supply problem: "The hardware industry supply side is used to growing 20% to 30% per year at most."
- Scope: chips, memory, networking, storage and full systems "from data centers to robotics to home AI appliances". It extends "all the way down to the electricity" (grid plus behind-the-meter) and "all the cooling, materials, electrical, and real estate build out to support them".
- Liquid cooling is not named specifically. Manufacturing is not a stated category.
- Named recent hardware investments: Unconventional AI, Nexthop, Volta, Atoms, Heron Power and Mind Robotics. Earlier ones: Skydio, SpaceX, Anduril and Waymo.
- Secondary coverage attributes these figures to a16z: rack power rising from about 5-10 kW to 100-250 kW and heading to 1 MW within three years, a 28x compute-density increase per rack from H100 to Rubin, and hardware above 20% of a16z deal flow — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/a16z-machine-age-fund-1-1bn-hardware); [Telborg briefing](https://telborg.com/datacentre-briefings/270) [secondary, not fetched; not confirmed in the a16z post fetch]

**Other AD context**
- A related AD "AI Edition" list exists — [a16z AD50 AI edition](https://a16z.com/american-dynamism-50-ai/) [not fetched]
- An AD film series (Aug 2026) profiled Ulysses, Radiant and Mariana Minerals — [StartupHub](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/investors-news/2026/a16z-spotlights-american-dynamism.md) [secondary, not fetched]

### Inferences
- (Inference, supportive) The asset-class essay explicitly invites venture capital into machine shops and component makers that co-engineer with systems companies. Cold plates are a co-engineered component sold to systems integrators: hyperscalers, ODMs and CDU vendors. The thesis therefore fits the "component supplier" layer a16z is describing, and Keil's "shift left to component suppliers" supports it.
- (Inference, disconfirming) a16z's manufacturing theses are framed almost entirely around defence and aerospace, with the Pentagon as anchor customer. A data-centre thermal component is adjacent to, not inside, that narrative. The AD team may see it as a Machine Age (infra) deal rather than an AD deal.
- (Inference, disconfirming) The asset-class essay says venture equity is "the wrong tool for expansion" and that there is no "software-style zero-marginal-cost escape hatch". Investors will expect a plan to move factory capex onto debt or customer prepayment early.
- (Inference, disconfirming) Keil argues that AI design is the easy part and that DFM, scaling and process data are hard. A pitch leading with "AI agents do thermal design" would read as the commoditising layer. The pitch should lead with closed-loop production, test data and yield.
- (Inference) "Cooling" appears only once, as a passing mention, in the Machine Age post. No sourced a16z statement singles out liquid cooling as a priority.

### Gaps
- No a16z essays specifically titled "software-defined manufacturing", "the factory of the future" or "autonomous factories" were found in 2026 searches. Big Ideas #16 and the asset-class essay are the closest.
- The Machine Age rack-power and density figures were not confirmed in the primary post fetch.
- The AD50 AI Edition page was not fetched.

### Inferences
- (Inference, disconfirming) Cold-plate design and manufacture is already a funded, crowded category. Frore ($1.6B), ZutaCore, Accelsius, Corintis (Swiss), MegaCool (generative-engineered cold plates) and DCX/LITEON are all active. A guide titled "Liquid Cooling Vendors After the Buyouts" suggests consolidation by incumbents — [wetheflywheel, secondary, not fetched](https://wetheflywheel.com/en/guides/data-center-cooling-companies/). A new entrant needs a manufacturing-cost or speed edge from the autonomous factory, not a design edge. This is consistent with Keil's "design is the easy part".
- (Inference, supportive) The investor appetite for "automated factory for one critical component" is proven at scale: Hadrian, Isembard, Senra and Podium. The cold-plate idea can be pitched as a "Hadrian or Senra for AI-infrastructure thermal hardware", bridging a16z's AD manufacturing thesis and its Machine Age infra thesis.
- (Inference) Machine-tending AI (Trener, RobCo, Vention, Standard Bots) is well funded as a horizontal tool layer. A "robotic machine-tending cell for high-mix shops" alternative would compete with these funded players. Running one's own factory captures margin they do not.
- (Inference) Data-centre robotics is early: Watney is at Series A and Exclaim at pre-seed, with performance still unproven. It is a less crowded but higher-technical-risk alternative, and it has a direct Swiss/Founderful precedent.
